Open or closed question: how to set your intention before a draw

We often think the quality of a draw depends on which cards come up. In reality, it depends first on the question you ask. A poorly framed question produces a confused draw, no matter how beautiful the cards.

Here's how to set an intention that opens something up, rather than one that shuts it down.

The reflex to avoid: the closed question

The natural temptation is to ask "yes or no." Will I find work again? Will they come back? Should I accept? These questions wait for a verdict. They turn the oracle into a decision machine, and put you in a passive position: waiting for someone else to decide for you.

The problem is that an oracle doesn't predict the future. A closed question forces it to answer beside the point, and you come away frustrated — or, worse, falsely reassured.

✗ "Should I quit my job?"
✓ "What's actually keeping me in this job?"

The right direction: the open question

An open question often begins with how, what, why, what about. It doesn't ask for a verdict but for insight. It keeps you as the active party in your own situation. Instead of "will I succeed?", try "what, within me, supports or holds back this project?" The first waits for fate; the second opens up work to be done.

Precise, but not locked down

A good intention is precise about the subject, but open about the answer. "What's going on in my life right now?" is too vague: the oracle doesn't know where to look. "How should I approach the tension with my sister?" is precise about the subject, open about the outcome. That's the right balance.

✗ "What does life have in store for me?" (too vague)
✓ "What's asking for my attention in my relationship to my work?"

One question at a time

Stacking several questions into a single intention ("Should I move, and will my relationship last, and is this the right time to change careers?") scatters the draw. Each question deserves its own moment. At Oradia, the Tore actually invites you to set a single, open intention, precisely for this reason.

The final test

Before you draw, reread your question and ask yourself: am I looking for someone to decide for me, or to see more clearly? If it's the first, rephrase it. A well-set intention doesn't take away your freedom — it gives it back to you. That's where the oracle, like a good personal development tool, does its real work.
